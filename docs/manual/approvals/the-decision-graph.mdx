# The Decision Graph

**What it is.** The **Decision Graph** is a visual record of decisions your agents and team have made — what led to each one, who acted, and what it touched. It's a read-only view for tracing how a result came to be.

**When you'd use it.** When you need to answer "why did this happen?" — trace a single decision back through its precedents, scan decisions over time, or group them by policy.

## Trace a single decision
1. Open **Decision Graph** from the workspace navigation.
2. Pick the **One decision** view.
3. The graph shows the chosen decision with the inputs, actors, and earlier decisions that led to it.
4. Click any node to drill in. The graph re-centers on that decision and the address bar updates so you can share the exact view.

## Scan decisions over time
1. Open the Decision Graph.
2. Pick the **Time-series** view. This is the default when no single decision is selected.
3. Decisions are laid out in lanes by actor, so you can see who decided what and when.

## Group by policy
1. Pick the **Policy** view.
2. Decisions are grouped into columns by policy. Each card is tagged "landed" or "policy failed" so you can spot where a policy blocked an action.

## Inspect a decision
1. Click a node or card to open the side inspector.
2. It shows the intent, action, actor, time, policy result (passed or failed), outcome, pocket, and counts of inputs and precedents.
3. Use the **Explain** box to ask a plain-language question like "Why did this decision land? What changed since last week?" and get an answer in context.

## Tips
- You can filter by actor, policy, pocket, or a start date. Active filters show as chips across the top of the graph.
- Deep-link to a specific view by sharing the page URL — the decision, depth, view mode, and filters are all carried in the address bar.
- For a simpler before/after view of what a reviewer changed on an agent's proposal, see the **Paw Print** decision trail (a separate feed of original proposals versus reviewer edits).

## If something's off
- "No decision selected" in the One-decision view means you need to pick a decision first — switch to Time-series, find one, and click into it.
- "No graph data" or "No decisions in range" usually means there's nothing recorded yet for the current filters, or the date range is too narrow. Clear the filters and try again.
- The graph reads from a periodically updated record, not a live stream, so a brand-new decision may take a moment to appear.
