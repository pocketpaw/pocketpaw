# Account security and single sign-on

**What it is.** Two places that control sign-in safety. **Account & sessions** protects your own login — password, two-factor codes, and the list of devices you're signed in on. **Single sign-on** lets a workspace require everyone to sign in through a company provider like Okta or Google Workspace.

**When you'd use it.** Lock down your own account from Account & sessions. Set up company-wide login from Single sign-on.

## Reset your password
1. Open Settings, then **Account & sessions**.
2. Under **Password**, click **Send me a password-reset link**.
3. Check the email shown on the page and follow the link. There's no in-app password form by design — the change happens through the email link.

## Turn on two-factor authentication
1. On **Account & sessions**, find **Two-factor authentication**.
2. Click **Enable two-factor authentication**.
3. Scan the QR code with an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, 1Password, or similar), or copy the manual key.
4. Enter the 6-digit code the app shows, then click **Verify**.
5. Save your backup codes — copy, download, or print them. Tick the box to confirm you've saved them, then click **Done**.

## Manage your devices
1. On **Account & sessions**, scroll to **Active sessions**.
2. Each row is a browser or device you're signed in on. The current one is marked **This device**.
3. Click **Revoke** on any other row to sign that device out.
4. Click **Revoke all other sessions** to sign out everywhere except where you are now.

## Set up single sign-on (admins and owners)
1. Open Settings, then under Workspace pick **Single sign-on**.
2. Choose a provider: **Okta**, **Google Workspace**, **Azure AD**, or **Generic OIDC**.
3. Fill in the Issuer URL, Client ID, and Client Secret from your provider.
4. Add at least one **Allowed domain** (for example, `acme.com`).
5. Click **Save**. After the first save, click **Test connection** to confirm it works.
6. Copy the **Sign-in URL** and share it with your team — that's the link they use to sign in.

## Tips
- Backup codes are each good once. If you lose your phone, they're how you get back in — keep them somewhere safe.
- Turn on **Enforce SSO for these domains** to disable password login for matching email addresses.
- To roll the client secret, just re-enter it on the Single sign-on form and save. Leaving it blank keeps the current one.

## If something's off
- If you lose both your authenticator and your backup codes, support can't recover the account. Print the codes when you set up two-factor.
- **Test connection** stays greyed out until you've saved the configuration once.
- Removing SSO doesn't sign current SSO users out, but new SSO logins stop working until you set it up again.
- Only admins and owners see the Single sign-on page. Members get a notice that they can't configure it.
